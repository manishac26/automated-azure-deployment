# Final Report: Comparative Evaluation of Hyperparameter Optimization and AutoML Frameworks for Network Intrusion Detection

## 1) Problem statement
Binary intrusion detection on CIC-IDS2017: classify BENIGN (0) vs ATTACK (1). Compare untuned RF, Grid/Random/Optuna HPO, and AutoML (FLAML).

## 2) Dataset and source
- Source: CIC-IDS2017 MachineLearningCSV/MachineLearningCVE (8 files).
- Cleaned: 2498105 rows, 69 features.
- Class balance (clean): BENIGN 2072961 (82.981%), ATTACK 425144 (17.019%).

## 3) Data quality and preprocessing
- Headers normalized (spaces stripped), duplicate column renamed. Constants and duplicate column dropped. Conflicts removed. Exact duplicates removed. Invalid values imputed; see reports/dataset_audit.md.

## 4) Split strategy and leakage risks
- Stratified 70/15/15 full split and fixed 250k/50k/50k benchmark (seed 42). No exact hash overlap across splits. No timestamp; splits are random across mixed days/files (risk of optimistic estimates). See reports/reliability_analysis.md.

## 10) Limitations and future work
- Random splits may overestimate; day/file hold-out shows drop.
- HPO/AutoML compared on 100k subset vs 250k baseline (different train sizes) - qualifications noted.
- AutoGluon not completed.
- SHAP not installed due to env constraints; permutation importance used.

## 11) Conclusion
- On verified benchmark results, FLAML (XGBoost, 100k train) achieved test F1 0.9980 (PR-AUC 0.9998), outperforming 100k RF baselines; 250k RF baseline gives F1 0.9969. Direct cross-size comparison requires caution.
